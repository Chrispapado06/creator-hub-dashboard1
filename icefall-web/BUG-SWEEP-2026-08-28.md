## Method

Rebuilt the `health.mjs` headless checker (Playwright + the cached Chromium at
`~/Library/Caches/ms-playwright/chromium-1228`; `playwright-core` installed in
the session scratchpad, **not** added to this project's `package.json`).

Three passes, all against the running dev server on 5194:

| Pass | What | Routes |
|---|---|---|
| `health.mjs` | link-following crawl, 1440×900 | 348 |
| `MOBILE=1 health.mjs` | same crawl, 390×844 | 348 |
| `commercial.mjs` | enumerated audit of every page with a price and a button | 69 |

Plus `preview.mjs` (the second DEV-only tree, 10 routes) and `unblocked.mjs`
(console check with the real network on).

**Why the crawl and not a URL list:** crawling from the index pages makes the
dead-link check fall out for free — every `/app/…` href the app actually renders
gets visited, and anything landing on the "Not here" `Placeholder` is dead.

**Why not `networkidle`:** mountain and trek pages mount maplibre, which streams
basemap tiles from `server.arcgisonline.com` for as long as the page is open, so
`networkidle` never fires on 300-odd routes. The first attempt was still running
after 16 minutes and would have taken ~2 hours. **This is very likely what
actually killed the two previous runs — not sleep alone.** The rewrite blocks
off-localhost requests at the router, settles on the app's own DOM, and waits for
the app's own images to decode: 348 routes in ~9 minutes.

Note the consequence: the link crawl reached only **1 of 4** company pages and
**17 of 59** trip pages, because the rest are not linked from anywhere it walked.
Those are the highest-exposure pages in the tree, hence the enumerated
`commercial.mjs` pass. A link-following crawl alone would have missed Elite Exped
entirely.

## 4. layout-responsive — TWO REAL BUGS

Desktop 1440×900: **0 routes overflow.**

Mobile 390×844: **2 routes overflow horizontally.** On a mobile-first product
these are real defects; screenshots in the scratchpad (`social-390.png`,
`saved-390.png`).

### `/app/social` — 564px in a 390px viewport (+174px)

The worst of the two, and visibly broken: the search field, the composer, the
"Trending" tab and every post card are clipped, with body copy cut mid-word.

Culprit is the composer. `<textarea class="min-h-[44px] flex-1 resize-none …">`
renders 436px wide. A `<textarea>` carries an intrinsic default width, and
`flex-1` will not shrink it below min-content — the parent has `min-w-0` but the
textarea itself does not. The `<span class="ml-auto … px-4 py-2">Posting needs an
account</span>` (185px) on the flex-wrap row below compounds it.

**Fix:** `min-w-0` (or `w-full`) on the textarea itself, not only on its parent.

### `/app/saved` — 425px in a 390px viewport (+35px)

`<article class="flex flex-col rounded-card …">` renders 393px wide inside a
390px viewport. Inside it, `<span class="flex shrink-0 items-center gap-2">`
(106px, holding the "Saved" pill and the remove button) refuses to shrink.

**Fix:** allow the header row to wrap, or drop `shrink-0` from the trailing span.

### 6.2 Refund wording typed in rather than derived

**Corrected after first writing this up — the original reading was wrong, and
the correction matters.** The first pass recorded "two contradictory refund
promises: 60 days on `/app`, 14 days on `/preview`, and the money model gives
only 25% at 14". That was a misreading, caught while fixing it.

`money/model.ts` defines **two** policies, for two different products:

| Policy | Terms | Governs |
|---|---|---|
| `STANDARD_POLICY` | tiered — 100% at 60 days, 50% at 30, 25% at 14 | an expedition |
| `FLEXIBLE_POLICY` | 100% to 14 days, then nothing | booking a guide by the day |

So "free cancellation up to 14 days" on the guide screens was **the right number
all along** — it is `FLEXIBLE_POLICY`, correctly applied to a guide booking. The
two windows are not a contradiction; they are two products.

The real defect is smaller and still worth fixing: that sentence was **typed in
as prose in three places** (`screens/GuideDetail.tsx:135`,
`screens/BookingConfirm.tsx:158` and `:334`) with nothing tying it to the policy
it described, so it would silently go stale the moment the policy moved. `/app`
already reads its equivalent off `STANDARD_POLICY`; the preview tree did not.

**Lesson worth keeping:** a number appearing twice is not automatically a
contradiction. Check whether the two sites are describing the same thing before
making them agree — "fixing" this by pointing the guide screens at
`STANDARD_POLICY` would have replaced a stale-able 14 with a confidently wrong
60, and it would have looked like a cleanup.

### The mobile overflow was a systemic CSS trap, not two one-offs

The first diagnosis — a `<textarea>` with `flex-1` and no `min-w-0` — was wrong,
and measuring the ancestor chain rather than the leaf is what showed it. The
container was *already* 532px before the textarea had any say.

The real cause: `<div className="grid gap-6 xl:grid-cols-[…]">` has **no base
column definition**, so below `xl` there is no explicit track and the implicit
one sizes to `auto` — and an `auto` track will not shrink below its content's
min-content width. Tailwind's `grid-cols-1` is `repeat(1, minmax(0, 1fr))`, and
the `minmax(0, …)` is what lets the track go narrower than its content so the
content wraps instead.

**59 grids in this tree have that shape.** Only these two overflowed, because
everywhere else the content happens to fit — they are each one wide child away
from the same bug. Seven were fixed (the five sharing the sidebar idiom, plus
Saved's two card grids); the remaining 52 were deliberately left alone rather
than blind-edited, since each changes how a real page reflows and the sweep found
no defect in them. Worth a dedicated pass with eyes on the result.

## Later the same day — the guide commission model changed under this tree

Separate work from the sweep, recorded here because it touches prices.

**What changed.** The owner replaced the 5%-added-on-top service fee with a
**10% commission deducted from the guide's fee**. The worked example is the
authority: a guide charges €1,000, the climber pays €1,000, the guide receives
€900, ICEFALL keeps €100. The advertised price is what is paid; nothing is added
at checkout.

**Why it reached this tree late.** `icefall-web` was never in `icefall-shared`'s
`npm run sync` list, so `src/money/model.ts` had been a stale copy since 25 Aug.
Session 03 found the omission and fixed the list. `priceBooking`, `Pricing` and
`SERVICE_FEE_PCT` were **deleted rather than deprecated** — the right call, since
a red typecheck is loud and a wrong price is silent.

**Scope correction worth keeping.** `tsc` reported 3 errors, both at import
sites, which reads like a three-line fix. It is not: the broken `Pricing` import
was **masking** property errors downstream. The real commit is four files —
`lib/trip.ts`, `screens/GuideDetail.tsx`, `screens/BookingConfirm.tsx`,
`screens/Plan.tsx` — plus prose in `lib/flights.ts`. Anyone budgeting "three
imports" leaves the typecheck red.

**The design decision, and why.** Three approaches were generated and judged by
two independent lenses, which split. The winner: **`tripCost` computes no
commission at all.** `TripCost.guide: Pricing` became `TripCost.guidingCents:
Cents`, and the guiding figure is simply `dayRate × days`.

The reasoning that decided it — under a deducted model, what the climber pays
does not depend on the commission, so the checkout path should not react to it.
Carrying `commission` and `guideReceives` on the checkout type with **zero
consumers in the tree** (verified by grep) and a comment saying "never render
this" is hidden-at-render, not gated-at-definition. Two further findings settled
it:

- `QuoteTotals.perPerson` is *guiding-only* per person and would have sat beside
  a *bundle* total on a screen that already prints "total · N climbers" —
  €500 against a real €1,340. An active misleading-number hazard, not a lost
  feature.
- The alternative required threading a `departureIso` into `Plan.tsx`, whose
  call site has no date (`startD` is `Date | null`). The nearest fallback is
  `new Date()` — inventing a departure to satisfy a field nothing reads.

The losing-but-valuable argument is recorded: if `totalsFor`'s notion of a total
ever stops being a naive sum of lines, this file diverges silently. Mitigated by
`icefall-sessions/requests/03-money-totals-for-flat-amount.md`, which asks the
money model's owner for a flat-amount entry point, and by a pointer in
`trip.ts`'s header telling the next developer to call it rather than hand-write a
split.

| Screen | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Guide listing | 3-row breakdown: guiding, "ICEFALL service fee", total | The day rate, plus `GUIDE_FEE_DISCLOSURE` |
