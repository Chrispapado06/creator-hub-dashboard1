# ICEFALL — objective requirements, for review

**What this is.** ICEFALL currently judges how ready somebody is for a mountain
from its **elevation alone**. Every 4,478 m peak on earth is given the same
requirements as the Matterhorn, because elevation is the only thing the app
knows how to compare against. The written requirements on each mountain page —
reproduced below, unedited — are display text: nothing reads them.

**What we are asking for.** Turn those sentences into figures the app can check
a person against. Once two or more certified guides have signed a mountain's
sheet, ICEFALL stops using the elevation band for that mountain and compares
against what you wrote instead. Until then it keeps using the band **and says
so on the screen**.

**What we are not asking for.** Nothing here is a route description or a
guarantee. The app never tells anyone they are ready to climb; it tells them
which of your lines its record of them does and does not reach, and it says on
every screen that the assessment that counts is made in person by you.

---

## How to fill a section in

Each mountain below has a table with one row per requirement. Add as many rows
as the objective needs and delete the rest. Every row needs all six columns.

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **Kind** | One of the eight kinds in the next table. |
| **Figure** | The number or value, in that kind's unit. |
| **Required / recommended** | `required` means falling short of it stops the objective. If everything is `required`, the field stops meaning anything — please choose. |
| **Why** | One sentence: why this mountain asks for it. It is shown to the athlete beside your figure. |
| **Source** | Where the figure comes from: your own judgement, an operator's published prerequisites, a named guidebook, or a permit authority. Name it. |
| **Notes** | Anything we should know — seasonal caveats, "this is for the normal route only", a figure you are uneasy about. |

### The eight kinds

| Kind | Unit | What it means | Can ICEFALL check it today? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `skill` | one of the competences below | A competence the climber must hold. | Yes — against what they have told us. Self-declared, and always labelled as such. |
| `altitude-reached` | metres | The climber must already have been this high. | Yes — from recorded sessions, marked summits, or their own answer. |
| `single-day-ascent` | metres | A single day of this much ascent, demonstrated in training. | Yes — from recorded activity. |
| `single-day-duration` | hours | This many hours on the move in one day. | Yes — from recorded activity. |
| `weekly-ascent` | metres per week | Weekly ascent held across a build. | Yes — from recorded activity. |
| `prior-summits` | a count, plus a height | e.g. two prior summits at or above 4,000 m. | Yes — but only summits the climber has marked in the app. |
| `technical-grade` | a grade, plus its scale | e.g. AD overall, or UIAA IV. | **No.** ICEFALL holds no record of what anyone has climbed or led, and will not guess from training. It will show your grade and say it cannot check it. Please still write it down. |
| `certification` | the name of a course or qualification | e.g. a glacier travel and crevasse rescue course. | **Not yet.** Certificate upload is planned. Until then ICEFALL shows it and says it cannot check it. |

## Signing a mountain off

At the bottom of each section:

- **Reviewers** — at least two, with your full name, your certification as
  awarded (e.g. "IFMGA/UIAGM Mountain Guide"), and the body that awarded it.
  A licence number if you give one. ICEFALL shows these names beside the
  requirements: an athlete is entitled to know who set the bar.
- **Date** — the day you reviewed it.
- **Scope** — what exactly you are signing. "Hörnli ridge, summer conditions"
  is a scope; "the Matterhorn" is not. The app shows this too.

A mountain you are **not** willing to put figures on is a valid answer. Sign it
off with an empty table and say why in the notes; ICEFALL will keep using the
elevation band and keep saying so.

## Broad Peak — 8,051 m

`broad-peak` · Karakoram (Baltoro Muztagh) · Pakistan / China · High-altitude expedition · typically 45-55 days round trip from Islamabad · permit issued to an operator, not to a person · ICEFALL marks this as needing a certified guide or operator

**Status:** No certified guide has been appointed to review this mountain's requirements yet, so ICEFALL holds no figures for it.

### Routes ICEFALL holds

| Route | Grade as written | Ascent | Distance | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| West Spur (Normal Route) | Snow/ice expedition route (no single consensus alpine grade) | 3,201 m | 8 km | 35-40 days on the mountain within a 45-55 day expedition |
| Northwest Ridge (Traverse of the North, Central and Main Summits; Kukuczka-Kurtyka, 1984) | Alpine-style traverse; very difficult technical climbing on the North Summit (no published consensus grade) | 3,600 m | 12 km | 13-18 July on the 1984 ascent; about 4.5 days from the base of the ridge to the main summit, plus a day to descend |

### What ICEFALL currently says — unedited, for your correction

**Technical requirements**

- Fixed-rope ascending and descending on 40-50 degree snow and iced rock, in double boots with crampons
- Snow-stability and avalanche assessment on the slopes between Camp 1, Camp 2 and Camp 3 and on the approach to the col, and the discipline to stand down when they are loaded
- Competent cramponing and self-arrest on hard neve and exposed traverses
- Crevasse rescue and roped glacier travel on the Godwin-Austen Glacier
- Navigation and route-finding on the summit ridge in whiteout, where the fore-summit and true summit are not distinguishable by feel
- Ability to function and make turnaround decisions above 8,000 m without supplementary oxygen, which most parties on this peak do not carry
- Cold-weather bivouac and self-rescue skills for a summit day that commonly runs 15-30 hours from Camp 3

**Required experience**

> Previous 8,000 m summit, or at minimum a successful 7,000 m expedition with high-camp rotations. Not a first 8,000er for anyone without prior experience of independent decision-making in the death zone.

**Training requirements**

- Twelve months or more of structured aerobic base building; capacity to carry 20 kg for consecutive long days at altitude
- Multi-week high-altitude acclimatisation history; prior nights above 7,000 m strongly preferred
- Strength and endurance work targeting sustained 15-30 hour summit-day efforts
- Rehearsed fixed-rope technique with mitts on, so it is automatic when hypoxic
- Formal avalanche training and practised companion rescue, including transceiver and probe use on a loaded slope
- Cold tolerance training and tested layering system for temperatures well below -30 C with Karakoram wind

## Kilimanjaro — 5,895 m

`kilimanjaro` · Eastern Rift mountains · Tanzania · High-altitude trek · typically 6-9 days on the mountain · permit issued to an operator, not to a person · ICEFALL marks this as needing a certified guide or operator

**Status:** No certified guide has been appointed to review this mountain's requirements yet, so ICEFALL holds no figures for it.

### Routes ICEFALL holds

| Route | Grade as written | Ascent | Distance | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Machame Route | Non-technical trek | 4,255 m | 62 km | 6-8 days (7 recommended) |
| Lemosho Route | Non-technical trek | 3,535 m | 70 km | 7-9 days (8 standard) |

### What ICEFALL currently says — unedited, for your correction

**Technical requirements**

- No technical climbing: no rope, no crampons, no ice axe on the standard trekking routes
- Sustained hiking on scree, volcanic rock and loose trail, including a short scramble at the Barranco Wall (hands used, no protection)
- Ability to walk 6-8 hours a day for a week at altitude, and 10-14 hours on summit day
- Tolerance of severe cold: summit night is walked in the dark at roughly -7 to -29 C before wind chill, and hypothermia and frostbite are real risks. A full insulated system with hand and face protection is mandatory, not optional
- Recognition and self-reporting of AMS symptoms; awareness of HAPE and HACE and the need to descend
- Sure footing on loose scree while exhausted, for the pre-dawn descent from Stella Point where most falls happen

**Required experience**

> Multi-day hiking on rough ground with a daypack, and the fitness for a 10-14 hour summit push starting around midnight. No rope, axe or crampon skills are needed. Prior nights above 3,000 m are useful but not required; altitude tolerance and cold management, not technique, decide the outcome.

**Training requirements**

- Regular multi-hour hill walking with a loaded daypack in the months before the trip
- Aerobic base training (running, cycling, stair work) sufficient for consecutive long days
- Practice with back-to-back walking days to test recovery
- Testing the full summit-night layering system, including gloves and face cover, in cold conditions before departure
- Where possible, a prior night or two above 3,000 m to gauge personal altitude response
